How To Work
With It

You say what you want to end up with. Claude works out how, and shows you the plan before it touches anything.
Keala's six steps About 35 minutes

Most people tell Claude what to do, step by step, then spend hours fixing what comes back. It's quicker to do it the other way round.

This is how Keala gets Claude to build things, and he uses it for nearly everything now. He hates reading plans. He'll also tell you that time spent in the plan is far cheaper than building, rebuilding and rebuilding again. You use less of your Claude allowance, you've got less to reread, and the result's better.

This workbook got made this way. I talked at Claude for a few minutes about what I wanted, it came back with a plan, and I sent it back with my changes. Nothing had been built at that point, so the changes cost me nothing.

The Six Steps

1

Give it the outcome, not the process

Say what you want to end up with, in plain words, with the context around it. Keep the how out of it. You stay clear on the intention and Claude works out the mechanism. Talk it through like you're explaining it to a person: here's my situation, here's where I want to get to, and here's why.

A way to startHere's what I want to end up with: [the outcome, in plain words]. Here's the situation right now: [what's happening, what you've got, what's not working]. It matters because: [why you want it]. Tell me how you would do this.
2

Start in Plan mode

Before you send it, switch to Plan mode. You'll find the mode setting in the row under the message box. In Plan mode Claude can look around and think, but it can't change anything.

3

Read the whole plan

This is the annoying bit. Read it anyway. Wherever you know something it doesn't, tell it, and get the plan changed.

4

When you can't improve it, get it to attack its own plan

Find the holesWhat are the potential holes in the plan you've developed so far? If you were me, knowing the outcome I want is [say it again in one line], how would you improve this plan to increase the odds we get there?
5

Pick the good ideas and bin the rest

It'll come back with a list. Some ideas will be good and some will be rubbish. Tell it straight: "Do 1, 3 and 6. Skip the rest, they don't make sense." Go round again until you're both looking at the same plan.

6

Then let it build

Approve the plan and leave it alone for a bit. If you trust the plan, set the mode to Auto so it doesn't stop to ask about every small thing.

One safety line

Letting it run is fine for work inside your Claude folder. Anything that sends an email, publishes something, spends money or deletes something, you check first. And leave Bypass permissions alone. It removes the safety checks, and you've got no reason to use it.

When To Skip The Plan

Quick questions don't need any of this. "How's this ad doing?" or "Fix the typo in that email" can go straight in. If it's going to build something, it starts in Plan mode.

You're The Boss

It's normal to feel strange about AI seeing your stuff. So decide exactly what it sees.

You wouldn't give a new team member your bank login on day one. You'd start them on something small, see how they do, and give them more as they earn it. Treat Claude the same way.

I had this fear too. I didn't understand how any of this connected, and I didn't know a web page could hide instructions aimed at Claude. So I asked Claude to check my own setup. It found gaps, and we fixed them. You can do the same with the prompt below.

How Claude Gets Into Your Other Apps

There are three ways in. They all let Claude work in your other apps, and they work very differently.

Try first Direct connection (a connector)

A ready-made link between Claude and an app. You pick the app from a list, sign in once, and Claude can reach it directly without opening anything. It's the fastest and tidiest way in. Gmail, Google Drive and Google Calendar all work like this.

To add one, click the + by the message box and choose Add connectors.

If it's not on the list MCP

MCP is the plug that connectors are built on. You'll hear the word when an app has a plug for Claude that isn't on the ready-made list, and you add it yourself with a web address the company gives you. My Meta ads account and Crib are both connected this way. Once it's added, it works like a connector, and people often call it one.

You don't need to know how it works. Tell Claude the app you want to connect, and ask if there's an official MCP for it.

For everything else The Claude Chrome extension

This one gives Claude a browser. It opens pages, reads them, clicks and types, using the sites you're already logged into, the same way you would. So if a site has no connector, Claude can usually still work in it. Most of my email clear-out was done this way, because my Hotmail accounts have no connector that works with them.

It's slower, because Claude is clicking through pages one at a time, and Chrome has to stay open while it works.

Be careful with this one

Claude is working inside your logged-in accounts, and Anthropic says plainly that this still carries risk. A web page can contain hidden instructions aimed at Claude. So watch the first few minutes of any new job, keep it away from your banking, and check anything that pays, sends or deletes. Read Anthropic's safety guide before you start.

Habits worth keeping

One job per session. Start fresh when you change topic.

If you don't know whether Claude can do something, ask it. Describe the outcome and say "can you do this, and how?"

Claude proposes and you decide. You approve the plan before it starts.
